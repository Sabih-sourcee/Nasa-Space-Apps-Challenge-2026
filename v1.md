# Beyond Earth: Version 1

This document describes Beyond Earth exactly as it stands on the `rahul` branch at commit `75e7cdb` ("Add mouse walking, real NASA maps and the Gravity Test Chamber", 15 November 2026). It records what version 1 does, how it is built, the rules and numbers it uses, and what is not built or not checked. It describes the code and does not change it.

Built for the NASA Space Apps 2026 challenge **Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer**, for players aged 7 to 12.

---

## 1. What version 1 is

A 3D astronaut mission trainer that runs in the browser. A trainee goes from an Earth academy to a shuttle flight, an orbital station, a lunar outpost and a Mars outpost. Version 1 has five parts:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Campaign** | Eight playable missions across five worlds, with checkpoints, star ratings, ranks and badges |
| **Mission Replay** | Go back to a decision checkpoint, change one choice and compare the two outcomes side by side |
| **Commander Reyes** | A three-panel comic briefing before every mission, radio calls during it, and a debrief that names a decision you made |
| **Crew Ship** | A shared, walkable 15-room ship for 2 to 6 cadets, or one cadet with three scripted crew members (Solo Training) |
| **Gravity Lab** | A glass test chamber that copies the gravity of the Moon, Mars, Earth, Jupiter and the space station |

Everything is drawn in code except the interface font (Exo 2) and four NASA globe maps in `public/textures/`. Play does not depend on any live API.

## 4. Architecture

```
src/
  sim/        Pure, deterministic rules. No React, no three.js. Everything here is unit tested.
  game/       Zustand stores (game, HUD, crew), the input layer, audio, the test probe
  three/      Canvas and fixed-step loop, astronaut, walking rig, terrain, sky, props, textures
  missions/   One scene per mission, plus shared lander, EVA, outpost, Crew Ship and Gravity Lab scenes
  ui/         Title screen, mission map, mission card, HUD, briefings, decisions, results, settings,
              credits, Crew Ship screens, Gravity Lab panel, buttons, icons and planet art
server/       crewService.ts: the Crew Ship mission service (a Vite plugin)
tests/        Vitest suites for src/sim
public/       textures/: four NASA globe maps
docs/         Plan, progress log, SRS matrix, demo scripts, science and credits, Crew Ship record
data/         Pre-event NASA data samples (DONKI, Moon illumination, Mars dust). Not used by the game
scripts/      Pre-event download and DONKI test scripts
```

How the parts fit together:

- **Rules live in `src/sim`.** Scenes and stores call these functions and never hold game rules of their own. The same reducer drives Crew Ship in solo mode, on the server, and in the tests.
- **Fixed-step simulation.** `src/three/GameCanvas.tsx` steps the simulation at exactly 1/60 s (`STEP`) through `useSim(step, after)`, separate from rendering. `simClock` can be driven by hand for tests.
- **One input layer** (`src/game/input.ts`). Scenes read named actions (`up`, `down`, `left`, `right`, `primary`, `use`, `brake`, `recover`), never raw keys. Keyboard, on-screen buttons and touch all set the same actions. Input is suspended while a dialog is open, cleared on window blur, and cleared on every phase change.
- **Stores.** `useGame` (`src/game/store.ts`) holds the screen, mode, save, the current mission run and its result. `useHud` (`src/game/hud.ts`) holds objective, meters, warnings, prompts and the touch layout. `useCrew` (`src/game/crewStore.ts`) holds the Crew Ship session.
- **Screens** (the `Screen` type): `home`, `atlas` (mission map), `briefing` (mission card), `mission`, `debrief` (results), `credits` (science), `crew`, `gravity`.
- **Briefing before the scene.** A mission scene is not mounted until the player presses **Take Control**, so nothing runs behind Commander Reyes's briefing.
- **WebGL fallback.** `webglAvailable()` and `CompatMessage` show a "3D graphics are not available" panel when WebGL is missing.
- **Test hook.** `window.__beyondEarth` exposes `game`, `hud`, `hudApi`, `input`, `simClock`, `probe`, `crew` and `crewPos`. Automated browser checks use it to step frames and read scene state. Normal play does not use it.

## 5. Campaign

### 5.1 Route and unlocking

The five-world route is **E02, F01, S01, L02, M04**. Each mission unlocks the next.

Three extra missions sit off the route and never block it:

- **F03** opens after F01.
- **S04** opens after S01.
- **L03** opens after F03.

A mission's status is one of Completed, Available, Locked or Planned (`missionStatus` in `src/sim/missions.ts`). After the results screen, **Continue** goes to the next route mission. A mission off the route leads on to the next route mission in map order.

### 5.2 The eight playable missions

| ID | World | Mission | Unlocked by | Phases (checkpoint name) | Milestone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E02 | Earth | Gravity and Suit Lab | Always open | `suit` (Mission start), `course` (Suit cleared) | None |
| F01 | Shuttle | Liftoff to Orbit | E02 | `checklist` (Mission start), `ascent` (Liftoff) | Flight Ready |
| F03 | Shuttle | Keep the Ship Flying | F01 | `ship` (Mission start), `dock` (Ship ready) | None |
| S01 | Station | Dock and Drift | F01 | `docking` (Mission start), `cargo` (Docked) | None |
| S04 | Station | Module Alert | S01 | `diagnose` (Mission start), `respond` (Leak found) | None |
| L02 | Moon | Power Before Shadow | S01 | `landing` (Mission start), `eva` (Touchdown), `outpost` (Inside the habitat) | Lunar Crew |
| L03 | Moon | The Last Battery | F03 | `fetch` (Mission start), `power` (Battery in hand) | None |
| M04 | Mars | Dust Storm Outpost | L02 | `landing` (Mission start), `eva` (Touchdown), `outpost` (Inside the habitat) | Mars Expedition |

**E02 Gravity and Suit Lab.** Walk to the suit station and judge four gauges. One reads a low 3.1 psi, which you report and fix by reseating the left glove ring, bringing it to 4.3 psi. Clearance stays blocked until every gauge has been judged. Then carry a pouch through Earth, Mars and Moon gravity lanes. The trainer keeps a lane's gravity until you land. Tiers: Junior shows safe ranges and has one fault. Middle has no hints unless asked. Advanced has two faults (suit pressure and the radio channel) and keeps the reference card closed.

**F01 Liftoff to Orbit.** Four checklist confirmations, a countdown, then hold Space to throttle up and steer through six gates. A guidance deviation starts once speed passes one third of orbital speed. Cut the engines inside the orbit speed band and hold for 1.5 s. Too many missed gates, leaving the corridor, or running out of fuel resets training to the liftoff checkpoint.

**F03 Keep the Ship Flying.** A four-room transfer craft with one dropped power line and nine tasks: inspect, take the connector, fit it to navigation or ventilation, check the air, run the backup air loop, grab, rack and latch the spare battery. Then dock with the S01 docking scene. Navigation powered gives docking a guidance display. Ventilation powered spends no air reserve but makes docking a tighter guided approach. Junior always sends the connector to navigation.

**S01 Dock and Drift.** Dock by alignment and closing speed, starting at 40 m. A poor approach backs off and holds at 25 m. Inside the station, push off, drift and grab rails to move a cargo bag to storage. A drift warning comes after more than 7 s of free drift. R reels the tether back to the last rail you held.

**S04 Module Alert.** Read three hatch gauges (Lab, Crew, Storage) and report the leaking module at the console. The lab is always the leak. Junior: the leak has the lowest reading and each gauge names its trend. Middle: the lowest reading is not the leak, so you look for the falling one. Advanced: a frozen storage sensor reads 97.0 kPa with a failed self-test, as a decoy. Then choose: seal the lab now, or first rescue the experiment with a two-joint robotic arm. Below 40% air reserve the crew moves to the shuttle and the mission restarts from the checkpoint.

**L02 Power Before Shadow.** An engine-only lunar landing, surface work (carry the pouch, deploy the solar panel), then habitat shifts run by the outpost engine (section 7). A shadow cuts panel output from 6 units to 2 while air support still needs 3. A separate solar particle alert, labelled with archived NASA DONKI alert 20260905-AL-007, offers the basic shelter (0 units, stuffy air), upgraded fans (2 units), or keep working (a critical outcome).

**L03 The Last Battery.** One spare battery and two sockets: the grow rack or the shelter fans. The clock moves only when the player acts, so a replay repeats exactly. The alert arrives at clock step 4. Full ventilation needs 4 units and low-flow mode needs 2. If the battery cannot run even low flow, or the crew waits outside too long, the shift stops and restarts from the Battery in hand checkpoint.

**M04 Dust Storm Outpost.** A Mars landing: open the parachute, release it, then land with the engine, with a primary and an alternate zone. Then inspect the rover (clear the wheel, brush the deck, take the pouch) and run the outpost through a dust storm. Solar income falls from 4 units to 1. Clean panels add 2 per shift, and backup cells add 2 once. On Advanced, a question to Earth is answered after the storm peak decision.

### 5.3 Planned missions (not playable)

Twelve catalogue entries show as **Planned** when you press **Show all 20 missions**: E01 The New Recruit, E03 Robotics Lab, E04 Countdown Rehearsal, F02 Rendezvous, S02 Science Lab, S03 Station Repair, L01 Landing Zone, L04 Hidden Ice, M01 Entry and Landing, M02 Red Planet Explorer, M03 Build Your Habitat, M05 Final Expedition.

### 5.4 Difficulty tiers

Junior, Middle and Advanced. A tier change takes effect at the next mission start or restart. Hints follow the setting: `auto` means on for Junior only.

### 5.5 Commander Reyes and carry-over

- Briefings come from `src/sim/story.ts` and are shown by `src/ui/Commander.tsx`. Each has three panels (where we are, what changed, your assignment), with different lines for each tier. Enter or the arrow keys turn the page, Esc skips, and **Take Control** starts the scene.
- **Mission Log** on the mission map replays any unlocked briefing.
- **F03 to L03.** F03 saves `batterySecured` in its mission record. If it is true, L03 starts with the battery on the near rack. If not, the battery is in the cargo crate, digging it out costs time, and the clock starts at 2. The L03 briefing says which.
- **L03 to Crew Ship.** If L03 ended with one battery unit or less, the Crew Ship emergency "The Air Is Getting Stale" starts with 60% reserve power, and Reyes says why.

## 6. Stars, ranks and badges

Rules are in `src/sim/score.ts`. A finished mission always earns at least one star. Training resets and checkpoint recoveries only cost stars. A replay never changes the checkpoint or the completion count, but a better replay keeps its stars.

| Mission | Three stars | Two stars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E02 | No misjudged reading or fix | 1 or 2 mistakes |
| F01 | Every gate passed, on the first launch | At most 1 gate missed and at most 2 launches |
| F03 | Battery latched, and docked on the first approach | One of the two |
| S01 | Docked on the first approach, and no tether recovery | At most 2 approaches and at most 1 tether recovery |
| S04 | No wrong report, and 60% air kept or the experiment saved | One of the two |
| L02, M04 | Air at 75% or more, and no checkpoint recovery | Air at 55% or more |
| L03 | Crew readiness at 100%, and no checkpoint recovery | Readiness at 65% or more |

Maximum stars: 24. Ranks by total stars:

| Rank | Stars needed |
| --- | --- |
| Space Cadet | 0 |
| Flight Trainee | 3 |
| Astronaut | 7 |
| Mission Specialist | 12 |
| Mission Commander | 18 |

Badges (milestones): Flight Ready (F01), Lunar Crew (L02), Mars Expedition (M04). The call sign is "Trainee" plus the first four characters of the guest id.

## 7. Outpost engine and Mission Replay

The habitat phases of L02 and M04 run on `src/sim/outpost.ts`, with scenarios in `src/sim/outpostScenarios.ts` (one per mission and tier, with ids like `L02-middle`).

- **Resources and their limits:** Air 0 to 100, Battery 0 to 6, Crops 0 to 4, Crew protection 0 to 100, Science 0 to 3. Junior shows only Air and Battery.
- **Turns.** Each shift has an income, a list of actions with costs and effects, and scheduled events. `validateSelection` refuses plans you cannot afford. The decision panel opens on the nearest affordable plan.
- **Delayed messages.** On Advanced, a question to Earth gets its reply one decision later. That wait stands for signal travel plus the time the team on Earth needs to answer.
- **Critical outcomes** are never saved as the checkpoint. Recovery restores the turn before the decision that caused them and counts as a recovery.
- **Mission Replay.** Every turn keeps a snapshot. From the results screen the player picks a checkpoint, changes a choice, and sees a comparison (`compareRuns`) of battery before the alert and the final resources, with the difference for each. Later choices are pre-selected from the first run. A replay of a replay still compares against the first run.
- **L03 replay** uses its own comparison (`compareBattery`) from the Battery in hand checkpoint, and the first run's route shows as dots on the floor.

## 9. Gravity Lab (Gravity Test Chamber)

Opened by **Gravity Lab** on the title screen. Rules are in `src/sim/gravityLab.ts`, the scene in `src/missions/GravityLabScene.tsx`, and the panel in `src/ui/GravityLab.tsx`.

- A glass tube (5.6 m tall, with a ruler marked every half metre) holds a cadet. Five worlds hang around it. Pick one by clicking its globe or its button. It opens on Earth.
- **Space** jumps (on the station, pushes off). **E** drops a ball from 2 m.
- The panel shows gravity, what an Earth scale would read for a 32 kg cadet, jump height, fall time and air pressure. A card says what happens to the body there and what you would need to survive. Each world's last jump and drop are kept side by side to compare.
- Fixed values: cadet mass 32 kg, push speed 2.6 m/s on every world, drop height 2 m, head room 3.4 m.

| World | g (m/s²) | Compared with Earth | Air pressure | Jump height | Ball fall time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moon | 1.62 | 0.166 | 0 (vacuum) | 2.09 m | 1.57 s |
| Mars | 3.71 | 0.377 | 0.6 kPa | 0.91 m | 1.04 s |
| Earth | 9.8 | 1 | 101.3 kPa | 0.34 m | 0.64 s |
| Jupiter | 23.1 | 2.36 | No ground | 0.15 m | 0.42 s |
| Space station | 0 (floating) | 0 | 101.3 kPa in the cabin | Drifts, bounces off the ends | Does not fall |

Science limits: no real chamber can switch gravity. Jupiter has no ground, so the tube only copies its pull. The body notes for the Moon, Mars and Jupiter are reasoning from a weaker or stronger pull, not measured crew data. The station figures come from NASA.

## 10. Crew Ship

Full record: `docs/CREW_SHIP.md`. Engine: `src/sim/crew.ts`. Content: `src/sim/crewData.ts` and `src/sim/shipTour.ts`. Interface: `src/ui/CrewShip.tsx`. Scene: `src/missions/CrewShipScene.tsx`. Client: `src/game/crewStore.ts`. Service: `server/crewService.ts`.

### 10.1 Modes

- **Solo Training.** One cadet with three scripted crew members. After each of the cadet's jobs, one scripted crew member does one job that belongs to their station (`botTurn`), so the cadet is never stuck waiting. Works without the server.
- **Host a crew / Join with a code.** 2 to 6 cadets (`MAX_CREW = 6`) through the mission service. Join codes look like `ABC-123`.
- **3D ship tour.** A solo walk with nothing at stake. Thirty pieces of equipment can be clicked, or walked up to and opened with E. Each card gives what it does, how it is used and the real crew word for it. **Words to know** lists all 30 terms.

### 10.2 The ship (the Kestrel)

Five rooms across and three deep, with the Galley in the centre. Every room in a row has doorways to its neighbours. Columns 1, 3 and 5 also have doorways between rows.

| | Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 | Column 4 | Column 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fore | Trajectory Lab | Bridge | Comms Mast | Observation Lab | Airlock |
| Mid | Power Bay | Life Support | **Galley** | Water Recovery | Cargo Lock |
| Aft | Propulsion Bay | Thermal Control | Grow Lab | MedBay | Storm Shelter |

- **Roles:** Pilot, Systems Engineer, Life Support, Logistics, Science, Communications. A role can only be held by one cadet.
- **Shared items:** reserve battery, CO₂ filter, water cartridge, meal crate, diagnostic card. Only one cadet can hold an item at a time.
- **Ship resources:** meal packs, fuel, reserve power, clean water, air quality, crop trays, crew readiness.
- **18 tasks** built on five kinds of panel: pick, order, count, dial and carry. Each task has a clue first (predict), an action (do) and a science card with a source after it (explain). A wrong answer explains itself, costs one clock step and can be tried again.
- **Crew calls** are preset only, with no chat box: Need help, Bring battery, Ready at airlock, On my way, Done here, Check the Bridge.
- **Names** are made up, for example "Cadet Vega-42". No real names are asked for.

### 10.3 Emergencies and drills

| # | Title | Kind | Shifts | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Ship Tour and Drills | Drill | 1 | None |
| 1 | Last Meals on Board | Emergency | 3 | Search the cargo (crew time) or ask for an earlier docking (fuel) |
| 2 | The Air Is Getting Stale | Emergency | 2 | Backup fans on the reserve battery (power) or close the bay (crew time) |
| 3 | The Last Battery | Links to campaign mission L03 | | Single-player |
| 4 | Water on the Wrong Side | Emergency | 2 | Recovered water to the crew tank or to the crop trays |
| 5 | Houston Replies Too Late | Emergency | 3 | Keep the relay powered or power the habitat |
| 6 | Docking Window | Emergency | 1 | Quick tight approach or slow guided approach |

Shift lengths shrink by tier. Junior is guided in order. Middle has free order. Advanced has the shortest shifts, higher costs and, in Last Meals, one extra fault. **Crew Replay** forks from the incident's starting state, keeps the first outcome, and shows the first room where the replay changed and what that did to each resource.

### 10.4 Mission service

A Vite plugin that runs inside `npm run dev`. It keeps one copy of each crew in memory and runs the same `reduceCrew` reducer the tests use. Restarting the dev server ends every crew.

| Request (`POST /api/crew`, `op`) | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `host` | Creates a crew and returns the join code, member id, a private token and the session |
| `join` | Adds a cadet to a crew by code |
| `poll` | Sends the cadet's position and room, and returns the latest session. The client polls every 600 ms |
| `act` | Applies one action from the cadet whose token was sent |

- Every request except `host` and `join` needs the cadet's token. The token decides who the action is from, and a token from one crew is refused by another.
- Actions carry an id and are applied once, so a repeated send does nothing.
- A cadet not heard from for 12 s is marked Away, and their claimed task and held item are released. Completed tasks stay done. Coming back rejoins from the server's snapshot.
- A crew is deleted when its session ends. Request bodies over 20,000 characters are dropped.

### 10.5 Not built

WorkAdventure walking and presence was inspected but not run, because the Docker daemon was off. No WorkAdventure code or assets are in the repository. The ship's own 3D scene does the walking and presence instead. Overlapping faults, clues spread across more stations, and anything that reads Docking Window's cargo record are not built.

## 11. Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Mouse or touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move, steer, aim | W A S D or arrow keys | Click or tap the ground to walk there, hold and drag to keep steering, or use the arrow pad |
| Jump, engine, throttle up, push off | Space | Large right button |
| Use, interact, grab, confirm, parachute | E or Enter | Use button, or click a station to walk to it and use it |
| Brake, throttle down | X (or B) | Brake button |
| Tether recovery, give up the arm rescue | R | Tether button |
| Pause | Esc or P | Pause button |
| Extra readouts | I | Info button |
| Sound on or off | | Speaker button |
| Dialogs and decisions | Tab, arrow keys, Enter, Esc | Click or tap |

**Click and tap walking** (`src/three/WalkerRig.tsx`) works in every walking scene: Earth, Moon and Mars surfaces, the F03 ship, L03 and Crew Ship. A gold ring marks the target. Clicking within about 1.4 m of an enabled station walks there and uses it. Any movement key cancels the target, and the keyboard always wins. Walking into a wall for about half a second gives up on the target.

Other control rules:

- The camera keeps one direction and only follows position, so up always walks away from the camera.
- On the title screen, the view leans with the mouse and the astronaut turns toward it.
- Each menu screen puts focus on its main button, so Enter moves on. On the results screen, focus waits for the stars and for any held key to be released.
- HUD buttons give focus back after a click, so Space and E keep working as game keys.
- On-screen buttons show by default on touch devices. The gamepad button toggles them.

## 12. Settings, saves and Judge Mode

**Settings** (defaults in brackets): difficulty (Junior), reduced motion (off), sound effects (on), spoken alerts (off), captions (on), graphics (High, or Low), hints (auto, on or off). **Reset Progress** asks for confirmation and erases only the active save slot. With reduced motion on, interface animations and transitions stop, the camera locks to its target, and flashing beacons and alert pulses become steady.

**Saves** (`src/sim/save.ts`) are kept in `localStorage`:

- `beyondEarth.campaign.v1`: campaign progress.
- `beyondEarth.judge.v1`: Judge Mode progress.
- Schema version 1. A save holds a random guest id (`guest-` plus 12 hex characters), settings, completed missions (tier, completions, summary, best stars, story flags), milestones, and one checkpoint.
- Nothing personal is stored.

How saves fail safely:

- Every part of a save is checked on its own.
- Corrupt settings fall back to the defaults.
- A corrupt checkpoint restarts that mission from its beginning and keeps every completed mission.
- An unreadable file starts a fresh guest profile.
- Each of these shows a note on screen.
- Old saves that had one sound switch are moved over with effects on, and speech on only if the old switch was on.
- If the browser blocks storage, play continues with a note that progress will not be kept.

**Judge Mode** is switched on from the title screen or the mission map. Every playable mission opens directly, mission cards gain **Start at** buttons for later checkpoints, and progress goes to the separate judge slot. Judge Mode uses the player's comfort settings.

## 13. Graphics and assets

- **Drawn in code:** every model, the astronaut, terrain, sky, props, sounds, icons, planet art and the mission patch.
- **Ground:** Moon and Mars ground has small craters with bright rims, dune ripples and stones, with matching relief so they catch the sun. Each sample is mixed with a larger, turned copy to hide the repeating pattern.
- **Earth globe:** starts with a painted Earth (invented continents, a shiny sea, a drifting cloud layer, a glowing rim of air). The NASA map replaces the painted surface as soon as it loads. A shine map is worked out from the real map, so water glints and land does not. The clouds stay painted. Seen from orbit, the globe is turned so the pole is not under the camera.
- **NASA globe maps** in `public/textures/` (about 2 MB together, used unchanged):
  - `earth.jpg`: NASA SVS 3615, Equirectangular Projected Earth. Used on the Earth globe everywhere and in the Gravity Lab.
  - `moon.jpg`: NASA SVS 4720, CGI Moon Kit (LRO). Gravity Lab.
  - `mars.jpg` and `jupiter.jpg`: NASA 3D Resources. Gravity Lab.
- **Font:** Exo 2 from npm, bundled. Only the Latin file is loaded.
- No NASA insignia and no flags. The project is not endorsed by NASA.

## 15. Known limits and unverified items

**Not built**

- Twelve of the 20 campaign missions are Planned only.
- WorkAdventure integration is not built (section 10.5).
- Only the player's astronaut is modelled in the campaign. Other crew are implied by text and lights.

**Simplified**

- Station movement and docking are on a flat plane.
- The robotic arm is a flat two-joint model.
- Station gauge readings are illustrative.
- The 18 Crew Ship tasks are short dialog puzzles, not separate physical mini-games.

- Real-time feel and pacing played by hand.
- Frame rate, especially with the larger textures. One reading on an integrated GPU was about 20 ms per frame on Low and 38 ms on High.
- Firefox.
- A forced WebGL failure.
- A physical touchscreen and a real mouse for click walking and globe picking.
- Two separate browsers or machines on one crew.
- Listening to the sound effects and radio voiceover.
- Showing the interface to a child.
- Crew Ship emergencies 2, 4, 5 and 6 and the quick games, which are covered by unit tests only.
- The DONKI alert page, whose host refused the connection.
