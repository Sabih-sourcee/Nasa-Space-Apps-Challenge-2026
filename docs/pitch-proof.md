# Pitch proof

Only numbers that could be checked against a public source are included. If a figure is a rounding of a source's raw counts, that is said plainly. Nothing here was made up.

## 1. Science labs in Karachi government schools

Use this line:

In Karachi, 1,588 of 1,914 government schools had no science laboratory, which is 83% when rounded to the nearest percent.

Do not say "the article said 83%." The article gives the raw counts. 1,588 / 1,914 = 0.8297, which rounds to 83%.

Source: Safdar Rizvi, "Facilities missing, students drop out," The Express Tribune, published 12 May 2023, 12:04 PM PKT.

https://tribune.com.pk/story/2416347/facilities-missing-students-drop-out

The article says it is based on internal documents of the provincial education department, a comparative review of the Department of School Education school census for 2021-2022 against the 2019 school year. The 1,914 schools are described as government schools in Karachi. Direct quote of the lab count:

"The damning comparative review documents further show that 1,710 of the total schools have no library, 1,588 schools have no science laboratories, and 1,528 do not have a computer laboratory."

Nearby figures from the same article, if a judge asks: 1,657 of 1,914 had no drinking water; enrollment in Karachi's government schools is given as 459,000 in 2019 and 395,522 in 2021-2022, a 13% drop.

Caveat: this is a newspaper report of department documents, not the census file itself. The census file was not found as a public PDF during this prep. If you can get the official census tables at the event, prefer those. Until then, cite the Tribune article and the counts it prints.

A nationwide "83% of schools have no lab" claim was not found in official sources checked here (ASER, IEA/TIMSS, ADB Punjab notes, older federal lab figures). Do not widen the Karachi counts into an all-Pakistan 83% line.

A related, different official figure, only if you need a Punjab comparison: an ADB sector assessment, citing the Punjab School Education Department Annual School Census 2022-2023, says only 60.6% of government secondary schools in Punjab have science laboratories (53.0% of girls' schools and 67.9% of boys' schools). That is not 83%, and it is Punjab secondary schools, not Karachi. Source: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/linked-documents/54298-001-ssa.pdf

## 2. Pakistan's TIMSS 2019 Grade 4 rank and scores

Use this line:

In TIMSS 2019, Pakistan's Grade 4 students ranked second to last among 64 participating countries in both mathematics and science. Average scores were 328 in mathematics and 290 in science. Pakistani students scored significantly higher only than students in the Philippines. About three out of four students did not reach the minimum international benchmark.

That "second to last among 64" is the same as 63rd of 64. Prefer the official wording, then add the scores.

Sources:

- Pakistan national TIMSS 2019 report, National Education Assessment System, Government of Pakistan, hosted by IEA: https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2025-07/TIMSS2019-National-Report-Pakistan.pdf

  Official wording: "The country ranked second to last among the 64 countries participating in the study." And: "Pakistan ranked second to last in both mathematics and natural sciences among all 64 countries participating in TIMSS 2019. The performance of Pakistani fourth graders was much lower than the one of England, Turkey, or Iran. Pakistani students only scored significantly higher than their peers in the Philippines." And: "Approximately three out of four students in Pakistan did not reach minimum learning levels in mathematics and natural sciences."

  Also: "Pakistan lags behind all the 63 participants countries or education systems, with the exception of one... Pakistani students only outperformed their peers in the Philippines."

  The Pakistan Institute of Education summary of the same report: https://pie.gov.pk/Detail/NTZmZjFkOTItYTczNi00Y2IwLWIwYzUtM2I3MjJiMWQ2Yzlj

- Official international achievement tables, TIMSS 2019 Grade 4:

  Mathematics, Exhibit 1.1: Pakistan 328 (standard error 12.0); Philippines 297 (6.4). PDF: https://timss2019.org/reports/wp-content/uploads/download/achievement/1_1-2_achievement-results-M4.pdf

  Science, Exhibit 2.1: Pakistan 290 (standard error 13.4); Philippines 249 (7.5). PDF: https://timss2019.org/reports/wp-content/uploads/download/achievement/2_1-2_achievement-results-S4.pdf

  The TIMSS scale centerpoint is 500. Pakistan's scores are well below that.

  The official tables mark Pakistan with sampling / reliability footnotes (population coverage note 2, and a reliability reservation in mathematics because a share of students had achievement too low for estimation). Say the score if you use it; do not hide the footnote if a judge is a methods person.

Pakistan did not have a TIMSS 2023 Grade 4 result in the sources checked for this note, so do not update the year unless you find a later official cycle.

## 3. What not to put on a slide

No rupee cost of "missing labs" was found that is solid enough to put on a slide. Leave it off.

Do not invent a before/after learning gain before you measure one. If you run a 5-question quiz with a few kids during the event, report the real n and the real averages, even if they are small.
